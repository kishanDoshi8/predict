# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Verdict (formely known as Predikt) is primarily for friends participating in private prediction groups. They use the app in a lightweight social context, typically around real-world events, and want to place picks quickly, track standings, and keep the game fair without overhead. A secondary audience is room organizers/admins who create and maintain room structure, moderate gameplay cadence, and keep the competition smooth for everyone else.

## Product Purpose

Verdict is a fun, lightweight prediction companion for friend groups. It exists to make recurring private prediction play easy to run, easy to join, and easy to stay engaged with. Success means sustained, repeat engagement around core competitive loops, especially duels and leaderboard activity.

## Positioning

Private-first prediction game built for real friend groups, not public markets.

## Brand Personality

Playful, competitive, and trustworthy. The tone should feel socially energizing without pressure tactics, and should consistently reinforce friendly competition over high-stakes gambling intensity.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble casino or gambling dark-pattern products. Do not resemble noisy, overloaded gamer interfaces where visual aggression overwhelms clarity and task flow.

## Design Principles

1. Competitive Clarity First
   Every screen should make the next game action obvious, with clear status and ranking signals that reduce ambiguity.

2. Social Energy Without Manipulation
   The interface should feel lively and engaging for friend groups, but avoid urgency tricks or behavior-shaping dark patterns.

3. Trust Through Rule Transparency
   Scoring, outcomes, and state transitions should be legible and consistent so players trust fairness by default.

4. Fast, Repeatable Weekly Flow
   Frequent actions (join, pick, check leaderboard, resolve outcomes) should be optimized for repeat cadence and low cognitive load.

5. Inclusive Competitive Play
   Core competition cues must remain usable with high contrast, keyboard navigation, reduced motion preferences, and color-safe signaling.

## Color Semantics

- Keep `primary` rose as the global action signal.
- Use activity cyan for timeline/card interaction context to avoid destructive ambiguity in activity feeds.
- Keep win/loss colors scoped to outcome semantics only.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Baseline target is WCAG 2.2 AA with explicit reduced-motion support. Interaction and status design should preserve clarity for keyboard users and for users who rely on high contrast or color-safe interpretation of win/loss and rank indicators.
